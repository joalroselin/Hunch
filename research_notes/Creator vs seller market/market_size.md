# Philippine market size: creators vs sellers

## Which market is larger and how should it be sized?

### Takeaway
Seller-side counts are more concrete and larger on directly comparable business-registration measures: the Philippines had about 1.237 million MSME establishments in 2024. The creator economy is economically large in aggregate, but official statistics do not isolate influencers as a customer segment; the best narrow count found was 15,625 Filipino Instagram influencer accounts up to 500,000 followers (Dec. 2024), which is not comparable to all creators or to establishments.

### Cited Findings
- DTI reports 1,236,908 MSME establishments out of 1,241,476 establishments in the 2024 Philippine MSME Statistics (99.63%). This includes micro, small, and medium businesses across sectors, not just digitally active sellers. [DTI statistics](https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-knowledge-hub/dti-statistics)
- PSA's 2024 National ICT Household Survey (reported in a regional release) says 15.4% of Filipino internet users sold goods or services online to earn money in 2024; this is an individual-level measure, includes services, and is not a count of registered businesses. The same release says 29.8% (27.20M) of people 10+ had either online buying or selling accounts; that combined figure must not be presented as a seller count. [PSA RSSO II 2024 NICTHS release](https://rsso02.psa.gov.ph/system/files/attachment-dir/SR-2026-006_NICTHS.pdf)
- PSA's official 2025 pilot estimate values the broad Philippine creative economy at ₱2.12 trillion (7.6% of GDP), up 6.9% from ₱1.98 trillion in 2024; it estimates 8.71 million creative-industry jobs. This covers many fields (including traditional culture, symbols/images, advertising, publishing, arts, and digital interactive services), not just social-media creators or Hunch buyers. The PSA says the estimates are preliminary and based on a pilot study. [PSA 2025 creative economy release](https://psa.gov.ph/content/philippine-creative-economy-expands-69-percent-2025-accounted-76-percent-gross-domestic)
- A 2025 Manila Bulletin report citing a December 2024 estimate says there were 15,625 Filipino Instagram influencers with up to 500,000 followers. This is a narrow platform/follower-band count and secondary-source reporting; it does not cover TikTok, YouTube, other platforms, micro-creators outside the source's methodology, or creators who do not self-identify as influencers. [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2025/09/04/influencer-industry-new-capitalism)
- Meltwater's Digital 2026 Philippines write-up says Philippine influencer-advertising spend reached US$125 million in 2025, up 15% year over year. This is advertiser spend in the influencer-ad category, not creators' net income, software spend, or a count of addressable creator customers. [Meltwater Philippines social-media statistics](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/social-media-statistics-philippines)

### Inferences
- On countable local entities, sellers have a clearer and larger starting pool; the actual reachable segment for a focused seller tool is the subset of MSMEs that actively sell online, use paid promotion, and can measure conversions.
- The creator opportunity can have greater revenue attached per successful brand campaign, but aggregate creative-economy GVA and influencer-ad spend cannot establish a bigger software market or higher SaaS willingness to pay.
- Compare both as serviceable paying customers (e.g., creators who earn consistently from brand/affiliate deals; merchants who run recurring paid tests), rather than comparing 1.2M registered MSMEs with a broad creative-economy peso figure.

### Gaps
- No official Philippines-wide count was found for monetizing influencers/creators, creators who buy creator-growth software, or creators able and willing to pay monthly.
- A claim of 500,000–900,000 MSMEs actively selling online appears in secondary reporting, but a current primary DTI/PSA source supporting that exact range was not located. Prefer the newer PSA survey measures with their limitations.
- The NICTHS release has potentially confusing wording around a 0.47M count and percentages: its figures mix national and Cagayan Valley results. Do not use 0.47M as a national seller count without checking the underlying national tables. The 15.4% national internet-user rate is explicit in the release.

## What market evidence suggests about demand and adoption?

### Takeaway
The seller use case has strong behavioral fit with Hunch's current paid-test/results loop: online commerce is measured, social sites are a widely reported selling channel, and the seller can tie spend to measurable outcomes. Creators have a real commercial ecosystem, but a generic “grow your audience” tool risks weak monetization alignment unless it helps them win/measure brand deals or affiliate sales.

### Cited Findings
- Among online goods sellers in the 2024 NICTHS, 94.4% used social-media sites to sell. The release reports this finding for online sellers, while the 15.4% national figure includes people selling goods/services to earn money. [PSA RSSO II 2024 NICTHS release](https://rsso02.psa.gov.ph/system/files/attachment-dir/SR-2026-006_NICTHS.pdf)
- The same survey says 36.7% of internet users aged 10+ bought goods/services online in 2024, versus 23.7% in 2019, supporting growth in the buyer-side environment rather than directly measuring seller demand for Hunch. [PSA RSSO II 2024 NICTHS release](https://rsso02.psa.gov.ph/system/files/attachment-dir/SR-2026-006_NICTHS.pdf)
- The Reuters Institute's 2025 Philippines profile notes younger creators and audiences are increasingly drawn to TikTok, while Facebook remains a critical network. This points to a multi-platform workflow, not a single-channel assumption. [Reuters Institute, Philippines 2025](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/news-creators-influencers/2025/Philippines)
- The US$125M influencer-advertising segment estimate indicates brand budgets in the Philippines, but it is money spent by advertisers, not by creators on creator tools. [Meltwater Philippines social-media statistics](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/social-media-statistics-philippines)

### Inferences
- Sellers are likely easier to explain and convert for Hunch's existing MVP: there is a concrete job (allocate a small ad budget among audiences and posts), a clear feedback metric (results per peso), and a plausible buyer with direct commercial upside.
- Creators may adopt a tool more easily if it improves a visible, frequent workflow (content ideation/production), but willingness to pay is uncertain when earnings are irregular and audiences are already served by free platform analytics and creation tools.
- A better creator wedge is “find which content angle converts to a brand or affiliate action,” not “guess who your followers are.” It needs a creator-specific outcome and measurement method before the current seller flow should be repositioned.

### Gaps
- Public data found does not compare creator versus seller software acquisition cost, churn, willingness to pay, or gross margin in the Philippines. Those require direct interviews, pilots, or paid-conversion experiments.
- Influencer ad spend, creator-economy GVA, and online-seller incidence are not directly comparable TAM figures: they have different units, scopes, years, and underlying definitions.
- No robust local evidence was found on what Filipino nano/micro-creators pay for analytics or content-planning subscriptions.
