# Choose Hunch’s first paying customer

Hunch should **keep product sellers as the primary customer for the current MVP** and test a narrower creator segment alongside them: creators who already earn through affiliate links, sponsorships, services, or their own products. The Philippines has a large creative economy, but that figure does not tell us how many creators will buy Hunch; the countable MSME base is clearer, and the seller workflow already connects paid promotion to sales-oriented outcomes. There is no solid Philippine evidence that creators pay more, churn less, or produce better margins. A creator-first pivot could become more profitable, but only if monetized creators prove a higher paid conversion or retention rate after accounting for new product and support costs.

## Big industry figures do not equal paying Hunch customers

The Philippines counted **1.237 million MSME establishments in 2024**, while PSA’s preliminary estimate put the much broader creative economy at **₱2.12 trillion, or 7.6% of GDP, in 2025**. These figures measure different things: MSMEs are business establishments across sectors, and creative-economy output includes work beyond influencers. Neither is a serviceable market estimate for Hunch. A narrow secondary estimate counted 15,625 Filipino Instagram influencers up to 500,000 followers in December 2024, but it excludes other platforms and cannot be compared directly with all MSMEs. ([DTI statistics](https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-knowledge-hub/dti-statistics); [PSA creative-economy release](https://psa.gov.ph/content/philippine-creative-economy-expands-69-percent-2025-accounted-76-percent-gross-domestic); [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2025/09/04/influencer-industry-new-capitalism))

The behavior evidence is more useful than headline TAMs. PSA’s 2024 ICT household survey says 15.4% of internet users sold goods or services online to earn money; among online goods sellers, 94.4% used social media to sell. That supports a real social-selling channel, though it still does not tell us who uses paid ads or would pay for Hunch. ([PSA RSSO II survey release](https://rsso02.psa.gov.ph/system/files/attachment-dir/SR-2026-006_NICTHS.pdf)) The estimated **US$125 million in Philippine influencer-advertising spend in 2025** is money advertisers spent on influencer marketing—not creators’ income or creators’ software budgets. ([Meltwater](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/social-media-statistics-philippines))

## Sellers match today’s test-and-results loop

Hunch currently starts with a product, proposes buyer situations, prepares product posts, guides paid tests, and compares outcomes. For sellers, the business question is concrete: which audience and post merits the next peso of promotion? For a creator who only wants more views or followers, the same flow lacks the right hypotheses and success measures. TikTok itself separates visibility/follower goals from sales and lead-generation goals, reflecting that these are distinct jobs. ([TikTok Promote](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-promote-on-tiktok?lang=en-GB))

Creators do already use social-management and analytics tools, but available Philippine survey evidence is preliminary: its article does not provide sample size or methodology, and reports that software cost and learning time are common barriers. Free platform analytics and low-cost scheduling tools also make generic calendars, captions, and analytics a crowded offer. ([Newsbytes influencer-tools survey](https://newsbytes.ph/2024/02/02/digital-influencer-influencers-embracing-ai-digital-tools-while-preserving-authenticity/); [TikTok Studio](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/helping-creators-bring-creativity-to-life-with-tiktok-studio?lang=en); [Buffer pricing](https://buffer.com/pricing)) That evidence argues against repositioning Hunch as another creator-posting assistant. A creator version needs to distinguish itself through tested content hypotheses tied to an outcome.

## Higher creator margins remain a hypothesis

A monetizing creator may have a stronger reason to pay than a hobbyist: Hunch could help improve affiliate clicks, sponsorship results, service leads, or sales of a creator-owned product. That is a promising segment hypothesis, not a proven willingness-to-pay advantage. The available evidence does not compare Philippine creator and seller software spending, acquisition costs, churn, or gross margin. Hunch’s model costs also depend on usage; a higher creator subscription price would improve margins only if it outweighs acquisition, support, and any added measurement or integration costs.

Adoption could go either way. Creators may be easier to recruit through existing creator relationships and may already understand content tools, but that does not show they will pay or run a paid test. Sellers have a clearer connection between campaign spend and business results, but an ₱800 test budget and Ads Manager setup may impede adoption. These are product hypotheses to measure, not established segment facts.

## Test two segments before changing the positioning

Keep the seller flow intact and run a small paired pilot with sellers and **monetized creators**, such as affiliate sellers or creators selling services/products. Ask both groups to complete the same hypothesis-to-post-to-results loop. Track completion, time to first useful result, whether they spend on the promotion itself, willingness to pay for another cycle, and repeat use. Compare those results before making claims about profitability or market share.

For creators, test language and outcomes that reflect their revenue path: affiliate clicks or conversions, qualified leads, or offer sales. Creators focused on organic audience growth need different success metrics—such as profile visits, follows, saves, and watch time—and a product change; don’t mix those goals into one winner score. The first creator test should therefore target creators with an offer and measurable conversion, rather than influencers in general.

## Conclusion

Do not make sellers secondary yet. Keep sellers as Hunch’s first paying-customer hypothesis because the current product is already built around their measurable sales test. Use monetized creators as the closest adjacent segment, and let pilot completion and payment behavior—not the broad size of the creator economy—decide whether they become the primary market.
